Last Colony: Game Guide
=======================

October 21, 2026

Game overview

Last Colony is a top-down 2D factory-defence strategy game in the spirit of Mindustry. You play the invader for once. The Nymbri Hive is a living swarm of aquatic plant life that takes root on the planet Nereth, and the Human Defence Force (HDF) is fighting to stop it.

The factions

- Nymbri Hive: a single organism spread across many bodies. The Seed Cluster is its collective consciousness, the shared mind of the whole hive rather than a commander. On each map it plants a Bloom, a giant waterlily-like heart that stores everything the swarm gathers and spits at anything close. If the Bloom dies, you lose the map.
- Human Defence Force: the military of Nereth's human settlers, led by Supreme General Varrick. It fights with industrial machinery: drills, conveyors, steel walls, turrets, tanks, drones and bombers.

The species

- Common name: Nymbri. Scientific name: Nymbria communis.
- The name is a human invention and only provisional. The HDF's Biohazard Division gave it during the campaign. The genus is named after Dr Elena Nymbris, the Division's lead xenobiological researcher, and "communis" refers to the way the organisms seem to share functions and behaviour. The hive has no name for itself.
- Every Nymbri creature is a plant, including the ants. Parts of their tissue work like animal organs: they digest, move and sense.
- The Bloom and the Seed Cluster are two views of one mind, not a leader and a follower. The hive fights to survive, not out of hatred.

How you play

1. Grow Root Taps on ore veins (copper, lead, coal, sand, titanium) and lay Vines that carry the ore to your Bloom or straight into turrets. Root Taps and Deep Roots need no Rootweb under them, so you can tap an outlying vein and run vines back to the Rootweb: vines may reach up to 5 tiles past it. A vine opens into a fleshy collar only where items really pass between it and a growth; a sealed, rounded end means nothing gets through there (for example a vine pointing into a Root Tap or a wall).
2. Spread Rootweb, the purple living carpet your growths must stand on. It grows outward from the Bloom and from Rootweb Nodes. Only the Bloom feeds it: Rootweb joined to the Bloom through other Rootweb is fed and pulses with nutrients. If a patch is cut off (say the Node linking it is destroyed), it stops pulsing and fades to a dull grey-violet over about 14 seconds. This is only a sign: everything on it keeps working, and its Nodes keep growing it. Join it up again and its colour comes back over about 6 seconds. Two routes are safer than one: while any route to the Bloom remains, the patch stays fed. Rootweb never grows over exposed ore: it grows round each vein and leaves the crystals open. Ore the Rootweb has grown round still counts as the hive's ground, so you can build on it.
3. Turn raw ore into better materials. Resin Glands digest coal into resin; Chitin Vats grow coal and sand into chitin.
4. Make vigor (power) with Sap Burners and Sunleaves to run advanced growths such as the Chitin Vat, Sunlance, Healing Pod and Brood Chamber.
5. Defend with spitters and bark walls fed by vine, then hatch ants and send them to Defend the Bloom or Attack.
6. Spend resources in the Evolution tree to unlock new growths.

Resources: Copper, Lead, Sand, Coal and Titanium are mined. Resin and Chitin are processed. Titanium needs a Deep Root.

Modes: a five-mission Campaign across the planet Nereth, plus two standalone modes, Survival and Base Siege. Optional extras are sound and ambience (with their own volume settings), fog of war, the HD Remaster look, painted artwork for the Bloom and Spawning Pod, save slots and save files, and a Test Range save for trying units and turrets.

Nymbri Hive structures

The swarm has 26 growths, including the Bloom. All of them except the Bloom, Vines, Swift Vines, Vine Knots, Root Taps and Deep Roots must be planted on Rootweb (or on ore the Rootweb has grown round). Every growth you place is paid for at once and then grows on its own: see Growing below.

- Bloom (Heart, 3×3): The swarm's headquarters on each map. Stores everything gathered, grows Rootweb toward 30% of the map and spits at nearby enemies. Lose it and you lose the map.
- Thorn Spitter (Spitters, 1×1): Twin thorns at ground and air. Fed copper, resin or chitin.
- Spore Flak (Spitters, 2×2): Spore bursts against flyers only. Fed lead or chitin. Its spore sacs draw taut as each shot comes due, and its launching neck puffs out a cluster of spores that bursts among the flyers.
- Seed Mortar (Spitters, 1×1): Long-range exploding seed pods against ground targets. Fed resin or chitin. Its muscular chamber clenches and throws a hardened seed that splits apart where it lands; an empty opening means it is reloading or out of ammunition.
- Sunlance (Spitters, 2×2): A piercing beam of light against ground targets. Uses 40 vigor. Its petals close round the lens and its veins glow gold while it gathers; a dark lens means it has no target or not enough vigor.
- Root Tap (Roots, 2×2): Mines copper, lead, coal or sand and pushes it into adjacent vines. Can grow on ore outside the Rootweb; it is not a source of Rootweb and does not extend the vine reach. Its tendrils pulse once for every piece mined, and grains of the ore gather in its sac; a tap that has stopped pulsing is backed up or has nothing to mine.
- Deep Root (Roots, 2×2): A faster tap that can also break down titanium. Heavier, slower pulses through its pressure chamber.
- Vine (Vines, 1×1): Carries items in the direction it grows. Units can walk over it. Vines, Swift Vines and Vine Knots may run up to 5 tiles beyond the Rootweb (see Vine reach below).
- Swift Vine (Vines, 1×1): A vine about twice as fast. Grow it over old vines to upgrade them.
- Vine Knot (Vines, 1×1): Lets two vines cross without mixing.
- Splitter Bulb (Vines, 1×1): Splits incoming items evenly between its other sides. It has no fixed entrance: any side a vine feeds is an input, and every other side with a vine leading away is an output, so turning the vines round turns the bulb round too.
- Sorting Gland (Vines, 1×1): Passes one chosen item straight through and sends the rest left or right.
- Resin Gland (Glands, 2×2): Digests 2 coal into 1 resin.
- Chitin Vat (Glands, 2×2): Grows 1 coal and 2 sand into chitin. Uses 30 vigor. When it is working the fluid turns and a new plate forms inside; when it is short of coal, sand or vigor it rests and the fluid barely moves.
- Sap Burner (Vigor, 1×1): Burns coal for 60 vigor. Its chamber glows amber while it burns and flares as each piece of coal catches; a dark chamber means it has run out of coal.
- Sunleaf (Vigor, 1×1): A trickle of 5 vigor from sunlight, no fuel.
- Rootweb Node (Rootweb, 1×1): Grows a new patch of Rootweb. Chain them outward.
- Spore Bellows (Rootweb, 2×2): Doubles Rootweb spread within 12 tiles. Uses 10 vigor. It breathes out clouds of spores while it is speeding up Rootweb that is still spreading, and rests when there is nothing left to speed up or the swarm is short of vigor.
- Bark Wall (Bark, 1×1): Basic wall that soaks up fire. Living roots woven through bark; walls side by side knit their roots together into one hedge.
- Great Bark (Bark, 2×2): Four bark walls in one. Overlapping bark plates bound by two great root bundles.
- Ironbark (Bark, 1×1): A much tougher wall hardened with titanium. Near-black, mineral-flecked plates.
  All three walls flinch and bleed sap when hit, crack and split as their health falls, and glow green while a Healing Pod mends them.
- Petal Gate (Bark, 1×1): A wall that opens for your ants. Too narrow for Bulwark Ants: they will not squeeze through and go round to a Maw Gate or another way instead, and the gate stays shut for them.
- Maw Gate (Bark, 2×2): A wide gate that opens for every ant, Bulwarks included.
- Healing Pod (Bark, 1×1): Mends your growths within 6 tiles. Uses 12 vigor.
  Both gates are living passages that knit into the walls beside them: the Petal Gate's two petals fold back and the Maw Gate's membranes draw open like an iris whenever one of your ants comes through. A Healing Pod breathes quietly at rest, and each time it really mends something it squeezes and sends a pulse of green sap to each growth it healed.
- Spawning Pod (Brood, 2×2): Grows Mite Ants from copper and lead. No vigor needed.
- Brood Chamber (Brood, 3×3): Hatches Thornback and Bulwark Ants. Uses 30 vigor. The ant you ordered grows inside one of its sacs as nutrients flow in, and its release opening parts on the side the new ant comes out.

Evolutions that aren't buildings: Rapid Rooting makes Rootweb spread 60% faster, and Spore Winds adds another 60% and gives Rootweb Nodes 2 more tiles of reach.

Growing

A growth is paid for in full when you place it. Its spot is reserved at once and it grows there on its own over its growth time; there are no builder units. While it grows it is drawn faded inside a dashed membrane with roots closing in from the corners and a growth bar along its foot, and selecting it shows how far it has grown and how long is left. It does nothing until it is fully grown: no mining, carrying, vigor, Rootweb, firing, healing or hatching, and a growing gate stays shut. It starts at a tenth of its health and gains health as it grows, so enemies can break it; one destroyed while growing returns nothing. Reabsorbing a growth (right-click, X or Delete), growing or grown, returns exactly what it cost. Pre-placed and story structures, and everything in a saved game from before this version, start fully grown.

Growth times: Vine 0.5 s, Swift Vine and Vine Knot 0.8 s, Splitter Bulb 1 s, Sorting Gland 1.2 s, Bark Wall 1.5 s, Petal Gate 2.5 s, Ironbark 3 s, Thorn Spitter 3 s, Sunleaf 3 s, Rootweb Node 3 s, Root Tap 4 s, Sap Burner 4 s, Healing Pod 4 s, Seed Mortar 4 s, Great Bark 5 s, Deep Root 6 s, Spore Flak 7 s, Maw Gate 7 s, Spore Bellows 7 s, Spawning Pod 7 s, Resin Gland 8 s, Chitin Vat 8 s, Sunlance 10 s, Brood Chamber 12 s. A dragged line of vines grows from the Rootweb end outward, one tile starting a little after the last.

Vine reach

Vines (and Swift Vines and Vine Knots) can run up to 5 tiles past the Rootweb. The count follows the shortest chain of vine tiles touching edge to edge back to the Rootweb: the first tile off the Rootweb is tile 1, tile 5 is allowed and tile 6 is refused. Branches, loops, Root Taps and other growths do not restart the count; vines that reach Rootweb again count from there. A Vine beyond the Rootweb must connect to it, directly or through other Vines. If a vine line is cut, the cut-off part stays and keeps working but gives no reach for new vines until it is joined up again.

Building controls

- Drag with a Vine or Swift Vine to lay a line. The preview plans the route as you drag: it bends round rock, water and other growths with as few turns as it can, points every vine at the next, and can run along an existing vine line (to upgrade it to Swift Vines or turn it round) but never turns vines it crosses side-on. Each tile is outlined green (will be placed), orange (not enough in the Bloom) or red (blocked or out of reach); tiles off the Rootweb show their reach number (5 in gold) or a cross past the limit, and small numbers show the order the tiles are paid for and grow. A label gives the count, the total cost and how many tiles cannot be placed. A drag that ends on the side of a vine stops beside it, feeding into it.
- Vines and Swift Vines grow in one direction. Turn a chosen Vine with the mouse wheel before you place it: each notch turns it a quarter, wheel down clockwise and wheel up anticlockwise (R and Shift+R do the same). The preview shows the way it will grow, with an arrow and a small green tip on the side it will feed. While a Vine is chosen the wheel turns it and does not zoom; with anything else chosen, or nothing, the wheel zooms as usual. Other growths look the same whichever way they face, so they do not turn.
- During a vine drag the wheel (or R) turns the line. A straight line turns as a whole, as in Mindustry: every vine faces the chosen way, which lets you lay a row of vines side by side that all feed one way, or reverse a line. A line with corners keeps them: one notch turns its end vine, and two notches reverse the whole flow so items run from the end back to the start. The label says "line turned", "end turned" or "flow reversed". While Ctrl is held the staircase keeps its own turns; let go of Ctrl to apply the chosen direction. Turning never changes the cost, the growth time or the 5-tile vine reach, and a tile that cannot be placed stays refused. When you release, the next Vine starts facing the way the line ended.
- Hold Ctrl while dragging vines to lay a staircase of orthogonal vines along the diagonal. You can press or release Ctrl part-way through a drag; the preview changes at once.
- If the Bloom cannot pay for the whole line, the tiles nearest the Rootweb are placed first, as far as it can pay, and the message says how many were skipped and why. Tiles that are refused cost nothing.
- Press Esc during a drag to cancel it: nothing is placed and nothing is paid. Esc otherwise drops the chosen growth or clears the selection, and with nothing chosen or selected it pauses the game and opens the menu.
- The pause menu (Esc, or Menu in the top bar): Resume, Settings, Save or load, Main menu, and in the desktop app Quit game. Main menu and Quit save the game to the autosave slot first. Esc closes Settings or the save screen and returns to the menu, and Esc in the menu resumes.
- Hold Alt to see a fine grid over every tile you could physically build on: open ground and ore, on or off the Rootweb, but not rock, deep water, growths or growing sites. Only the tiles on screen are drawn. The placement preview still says whether a spot needs Rootweb or is out of reach.
- Placement messages say what is allowed: most growths must be placed on Rootweb, only Root Taps and Deep Roots can go on ore beyond it, a Root Tap needs exposed ore crystals under it, and a Vine beyond the Rootweb can extend 5 connected tiles.
- F4 shows the enemy route view (for curious players and testing): the shared route field the HDF follow to your Bloom, structures it would have to break through tinted red, each enemy's role, target (red line), objective (yellow) and local path (orange), and a flash where the route field was rebuilt.

Controls

- Camera: WASD, the arrow keys or dragging with the middle mouse button. On a touch screen, drag with one finger when no growth is chosen. The mouse wheel zooms (unless a Vine is chosen). H returns to the Bloom, and clicking the minimap jumps there. There is no pinch-to-zoom.
- Left-click places the chosen growth or selects. With nothing chosen, drag a box to select several ants; click open ground to move the selected ants there.
- Ctrl while dragging a Vine lays a staircase route. R or the mouse wheel turns the planned Vine (Shift+R turns it back). Esc cancels a drag, puts the growth away or clears the selection.
- Right-click, or X and drag, reabsorbs growths; Delete reabsorbs the selected one. Q copies the growth under the pointer. Hold Alt to see the ground you can grow on.
- 1 to 0 pick growths quickly (1 Vine, 4 Root Tap, 5 Thorn Spitter). C or the Swarm button switches every ant between Defend and Attack. T opens evolution. Space pauses; the 1×, 2× and 4× buttons set the speed.
- The same list is in the tutorial panel (Controls) and in Settings.

Sound and the event camera

- Sound starts with your first click or key press, because browsers only allow a page to play sound after you interact with it.
- Everything you hear comes from something that is happening. The Rootweb creaks quietly as it spreads, and vines and organs squelch as they are laid and as they finish growing. A refused placement gives a dull thud, and turning a vine gives a soft click. Damaged growths tear, and destroyed ones burst.
- Organs sound only while they work. A Sap Burner roars softly while it has fuel and whumphs as it takes coal in. A Sunleaf hums and pulses while it makes vigor. A Spore Bellows breathes and puffs on each exhale. Root Taps, Glands and Brood Chambers sound as they draw, process or hatch.
- Ants scuttle as one shared sound however many are moving, and each kind fires with its own sound. HDF machines are metal, motors and guns: each kind of vehicle has its own engine, and you hear them before you see them.
- The world has its own ambience, which changes with the sector: wind, small lives in the soil, the low hum of the hive as it grows, and a distant mechanical rumble when HDF machines are on their way. A fight brings in a tense layer, which eases back gradually afterwards. Story lines duck the sound a little so they can always be heard.
- Combat is built from noise, not notes: HDF guns crack and thud with heavy breeches and recoil, and hive weapons snap, clench and discharge like muscle and pressurised sap. A hit sounds like what it struck: soft growths and the Bloom tear like membrane, Bark Wall, Great Bark (and the Maw Gate) and Ironbark crack progressively denser, a Bulwark Ant's bark plates crack, other ants crunch, HDF vehicles clank (heavier on a Siege Tank or a big hit), HDF buildings crunch, shells and stray rounds hit rock or soil, and flame or a Sunlance beam scorches. Shots that miss or run out make no impact sound.
- In a big fight the lesser sounds give way a little and a limiter keeps the output from clipping, so a nearby explosion still stands out over distant small hits.
- Settings, then Audio, then Sound test plays every weapon, impact and destruction sound, and shots paired with what they hit, through the same volume settings.
- Settings, then Audio: Master, Sound Effects and Ambience volumes from 0 to 100%, and Mute. Master scales the other two (Master 50% with Sound Effects 80% plays effects at 40%). The settings apply at once and are remembered.
- Important story moments pan the camera to what is happening and hold it there until the moment ends. The camera then returns to where you had it, at your zoom, and your controls work again straight away.

HDF structures

The HDF builds 18 kinds of structure. It plays by the same rules as you: its drills and conveyors feed its turrets, factories and generators, and cutting that supply starves its defences.

- Colony Core (Command, 3×3): The colony's headquarters. Stores resources, pays for every unit it sends and fires at anything close. Destroy every core to win a base attack.
- Duo Turret (Turrets, 1×1): Twin-barrel gun that fires at your ants and growths. The colony's basic defence. Looks: a compact boxy head on a turret ring with two separate slim barrels that fire in turn.
- Hail Cannon (Turrets, 1×1): Long-range artillery against ground targets. Looks: a heavy head with a breech block and one long, thick barrel that kicks back hard when it fires.
- Flak Turret (Turrets, 2×2): Anti-air flak. The swarm has no flyers, so it never fires and works as a tough obstacle. Looks: a raised mount on a 2×2 pad with four short barrels and a spinning radar bar.
- Lancer (Turrets, 2×2): A charged energy beam against ground targets. Needs generator power. Looks: a long head with capacitors and cooling fins and one emitter barrel; a faint cyan glow builds as it charges and the fins glow hot after it fires.
- Mining Drill (Mining, 2×2): Mines copper, lead, coal or sand onto conveyors.
- Pneumatic Drill (Mining, 2×2): A faster drill that also mines titanium.
- Conveyor (Logistics, 1×1): Moves resources along a belt.
- Junction (Logistics, 1×1): Lets two conveyor lines cross.
- Router (Logistics, 1×1): Splits a conveyor line between its outputs.
- Graphite Press (Industry, 2×2): Presses coal into graphite, the colony's heavy ammunition.
- Silicon Smelter (Industry, 2×2): Smelts coal and sand into silicon. Needs power.
- Generator (Power, 1×1): Burns coal for power.
- Steel Wall (Defence, 1×1): Basic armour plating.
- Large Steel Wall (Defence, 2×2): Four steel walls in one.
- Titanium Wall (Defence, 1×1): Heavier titanium armour.
- Blast Door (Defence, 1×1): A door in the wall that opens for HDF units.
- Heavy Blast Door (Defence, 2×2): A wide door for heavy HDF vehicles.

The Test Range also shows HDF versions of the Titanium Conveyor, Sorter, Solar Panel, Mender and Ground Factory, but the colony never builds them in a match.

Units

Ants hatch from your Spawning Pods and Brood Chambers. HDF machines arrive in waves, or are paid for by a colony core in base attacks.

Hatching: each ant grows from an egg. As soon as a pod or chamber holds the items for one ant, it lays the egg and takes those items, and the ant hatches after its hatch time: Mite Ant 10 s, Thornback Ant 15 s, Bulwark Ant 30 s (a Brood Chamber short of vigor grows its egg more slowly). A bar under the pod shows how far the egg has grown, and selecting it shows the time left. Items that arrive meanwhile wait for the next egg. When the swarm is at its limit, no egg is laid, and an egg that is ready waits until there is room. Switching what a chamber hatches gives back the items of the egg it was growing.

Rally points: select a Spawning Pod or Brood Chamber and click or tap open ground. Every ant it hatches from then on walks to that spot, and they stand side by side round it. A dashed line and a small flag show the spot while the pod is selected. Clear rally point (in its panel) sends new ants back to waiting by the Bloom. The Swarm button still gives every ant the same order, rally point or not.

- Mite Ant (Nymbri Hive, hits ground): Small and cheap, grown from copper and lead. Weak alone, dangerous in packs. Looks: the smallest ant, round-bodied with a big head and a pale leaf bud on its back, and quick short steps.
- Thornback Ant (Nymbri Hive, hits ground and air): Quick warrior that spits thorns. Grown from chitin and lead. Looks: long legs, a narrow waist and a crest of pale thorns raked back along its body, with a long thorn spine on its back.
- Bulwark Ant (Nymbri Hive, hits ground): Huge armoured ant that lobs acid seeds with splash. Grown from chitin, resin and titanium. Too big for Petal Gates, so give your walls a Maw Gate. Looks: broad and heavy, its abdomen armoured in bark plates, with crushing jaws and a glowing amber seed pod on its back.
- Scout Tank (HDF, hits ground): Light tank and the backbone of every wave. Looks: a narrow khaki hull on thin open tracks, a small turret with a slim gun, and a whip antenna.
- Rammer Drone (HDF, hits ground): Fast drone that rams a target and explodes. Looks: small and wheeled with no turret, a yellow and black plough at the front, red charge drums and a blinking red beacon.
- Flame Tank (HDF, hits ground): Slow, tough tank with a short-range flamethrower. Looks: wide and squat, with armoured skirts, red-banded fuel tanks on the back and a short twin flame projector.
- Siege Tank (HDF, hits ground): Heavy artillery tank that outranges most defences. Looks: the biggest, with very broad tracks, a large eight-sided turret and a long gun; it lowers outriggers when it stops to fire.
- Interceptor (HDF, hits ground (flies)): Fast aircraft that circles and strafes.
- Bomber (HDF, hits ground (flies)): Flies over walls and bombs your growths.

How the HDF attack: every wave shares one route field to your Bloom. It goes round your walls through any gap, and only when you have sealed it out does it pick the weakest point to break through; it is rebuilt whenever your growths appear, finish growing or fall, at most four times a second. Each machine has a role. Scout Tanks look for exposed vines, Root Taps and growing sites near their path and leave the route to hit them. Flame Tanks go for the nearest living growth they can reach. Siege Tanks shell your spitters first, then other growths, and bark last. Rammer Drones ram whatever blocks them. Bombers never waste bombs on bark, and Interceptors harass your ants and supply lines. While there is a way round, Scout Tanks, Flame Tanks and Rammer Drones leave bark walls alone; a wall standing in their path is broken. A machine keeps its target for a while instead of flicking between equal ones, and one that stalls for two seconds attacks whatever is pressing on it or sidesteps. Story scenes, such as the Landfall patrol, play out exactly as scripted.

You order your swarm with one button: Defend keeps the ants near the Bloom, and Attack sends them after the enemy. You can also select ants and send them to a spot: click one ant, or drag a box over several with the left mouse button (with no growth chosen) to select them all. Shift-click or Shift-drag adds ants to the selection and Esc clears it. Click open ground to send them; a group spreads out round the spot instead of piling onto one point.

Campaign synopsis

The campaign is five sectors on the planet Nereth, played in order from a spinning globe. It traces first contact through to the fall of the last human colony. In Missions 1 to 3 each side only talks to itself, and the Seed Cluster speaks to the humans directly for the first time in Mission 5.

How the campaign works: each captured sector keeps producing resources into a shared global stock. Evolutions are paid from that stock and kept for every later sector. You can return to a captured sector with your base still running, for example to mine titanium once you have Deep Roots.

The title screen: when the game launches, a title screen shows a dormant Bloom in a night-time marsh, with the light of its Rootweb pulsing outward. Press any key, mouse button or controller button to go on to the main menu. It shows once each time the game starts.

The main menu: the menu sits in a dark panel down the left, with scenes from the game playing silently behind it. They are a working hive colony, a quiet frontier with a passing HDF patrol, an occasional skirmish at the last colony's walls, and the colony itself at work. Each plays for 30 seconds before fading into the next; Next scene, at the bottom right, moves on at once. The play tips and controls are under How to play. Settings > Moving menu background turns the moving scenes into still pictures, and the menu also uses stills when your system asks for reduced motion. Up and down arrows move between the menu buttons, and Escape on the planet screen goes back to the menu.

The opening: the first time you launch Landfall in a new campaign, a short intro of about a minute plays first. It goes from Nereth in orbit to the meadow where a survey crew is at work, then down into the soil where something old is stirring, and ends where Landfall begins. Press Skip, Esc or Enter to skip it at any point; the mission starts the same way either way. It plays only once per campaign, and never for a campaign you started before it was added or when you load a save. To watch it again, press Replay intro on the planet screen. This does not change your campaign and puts you back on the planet screen afterwards.

Rootweb in the campaign: every sector has a fixed map. The Bloom's own Rootweb only grows within its reach (11 tiles in Landfall, up to 18 in The Last Colony), which always covers the copper, lead and sand you need first. Plant Rootweb Nodes to carry the Rootweb further, toward the bigger coal and titanium fields. With a build tool selected, a dashed ring shows the Bloom's reach and each node's radius. Short radio messages from the HDF and remarks from the hive appear in the message feed as the waves go on; they never pause the game. Survival and Base Siege have no reach limit.

1. Landfall: hold 10 waves

A quiet meadow where the swarm first takes root. The Bloom wakes and tells the Seed Cluster what lies beneath it. The Seed Cluster tells it to feed itself and grow defences, though it doesn't yet know what from.

A mandatory tutorial (waves are paused) teaches ten things, one panel each: move the camera, select the Bloom, select a Mite Ant, move it, switch the Swarm to Attack and back to Defend, the Rootweb (the only panel with a Next button), place a Root Tap on copper, carry copper to the Bloom along a Vine, plant a Rootweb Node where it adds new ground, and prepare a Thorn Spitter. Each step is checked by doing it: copper must reach the Bloom along a Vine (a Root Tap touching the Bloom does not count), the Node must be near the edge of the Rootweb, and the Thorn Spitter must be grown and fed copper by Vine before first contact begins. A hint line helps if a growth is still growing, the ore is wrong, a Vine points the wrong way or the Bloom is short of copper. On a replay you can replay the tutorial, including first contact, or skip straight to the mission.

The map: a green meadow. The Bloom grows in a hollow on the west side, hidden from a dirt survey track that runs in from the east past survey markers, a parked survey truck and a radio mast. HDF waves follow that track.

Then comes a scripted first contact with combat switched off. HDF Patrol 7 (Private Kessler and Sergeant Okafor) drives in along the track and finds purple growth that wasn't there yesterday. The camera pans to them as their engines come over the rise. They see a spiked structure turn toward them and back away without firing. Okafor reports to Lieutenant Castell at HDF Command. Castell escalates to Supreme General Varrick and mentions that Dr Nymbris of the Biohazard Division wants samples, because the footage isn't enough to identify the growth. Varrick orders a cordon, samples for the doctor and burning. The patrol withdraws.

The Bloom tells the Seed Cluster that the hard things are gone but one reached toward it, and it reached back. Waves begin. After victory, the Seed Cluster concludes the humans were tearing their roots out, not feeding on them, and sends the Bloom north-east along the trails.

2. Ember Fields: hold 15 waves

Scorched coal plains, with flame tanks and bombers late on. You learn to digest coal into resin, a stronger thorn ammunition.

The map: ash plains with coal fields, rock ridges and burned ground. The HDF's Ember Depot stands fenced off in the north-east corner, and its haul road runs south-west past an abandoned drilling rig and excavator. Waves come down that road.

Before the fighting, the Biohazard Division gives HDF Command its preliminary assessment. This is where the species is named: Dr Nymbris tells Varrick the provisional classification is Nymbria communis, "Nymbri, for short". She explains that the tissue is mostly plant but works like animal organs in places, that the organisms seem to coordinate, and that nobody yet knows whether they are responding to threats or looking for them.

At the Ember Depot, Corporal Adeyemi teases Okafor about being chased off by a flower. Okafor admits the stories are true: it grows fast, hates fire and the spiky ones shoot back. Command warns the depot that Nymbri growth is moving toward the coal fields, and Okafor explains that's what the lab calls them now.

After victory, the Seed Cluster notices the pulses in the air change before each attack. One calls and many come, and the Bloom is told to find the caller.

3. Outpost Kilo: destroy the outpost

The first base attack: an HDF mining outpost guarding the only pass through the Kilo ridge. Its drills feed its guns, so you cut its belts, outrange it with Seed Mortars and send in Mite Ants.

The map: the Kilo ridge cuts the valley in two with a single pass. A road runs from the pass to the outpost, whose drills, belts and turrets sit outside its walls. A containment laboratory stands behind the core.

Varrick learns Ember Depot has fallen. Dr Nymbris reports that the Landfall samples keep growing in containment and respond to each other across separate containers, and that the ants' tissue matches the growths. She suspects the Rootweb carries signals between them. She also points out that Ember had been burning Nymbri growth for a week before it was attacked, which Varrick doesn't want to hear. Colonel Ruiz is ordered to hold the pass. The Bloom follows the ore into the walls, as the Seed Cluster instructed: take away what keeps their weapons working.

After victory, the Bloom finds pieces of the swarm sealed away, cut open and kept alive in the outpost's labs. The Seed Cluster realises the humans are learning about them, too.

4. Frost Canyon: hold 18 waves

A frozen canyon with titanium in its walls. Heavy siege tanks advance along the canyon floor with bomber wings overhead. You need Spore Flak, Sap Burners for vigor, Chitin Vats and Deep Roots.

The map: a frozen canyon between cliff walls with a frozen lake in the middle. The HDF stages artillery and tents at the head of the canyon road in the north and has put barricades across the canyon. Titanium lies at the foot of the cliffs.

Dr Nymbris shows Varrick that at Kilo the swarm cut the supply belts first, then the ammunition, then went for the command centre. That is planning, not just growth. She can't call them weeds anymore, and she notes that every assault logged so far came after a human one. Varrick refuses to wait. Varrick broadcasts a warning on every frequency: withdraw from our settlements or be destroyed. The Seed Cluster partly decodes it (boundaries, going away, destruction) and decides not to answer while it understands so little.

After victory, the human signals all come from one place. Their last forces are gathering to survive.

5. The Last Colony: destroy the fortress

A full mirrored fortress with walls, blast doors, Lancers and a working economy. Varrick makes a final stand here, with soldiers and evacuated civilians behind the walls.

The map: the valley is mirrored, so the colony's defences and economy match your side. Behind its core is a town with streets, 40 houses, HDF Command, a landing pad and three underground shelters. First contact plays from HDF Command, and the ending shows the shelters.

The Seed Cluster breaks into the HDF command band, and the two sides speak for the first time. It calls itself "many roots, one hunger" and says the humans call it their enemy. It never uses the name the humans gave it. Varrick accuses it of killing HDF soldiers. The Seed Cluster answers that the humans burned and cut the swarm first. Varrick begs it to turn back for the families inside. The Seed Cluster replies that it turned away before and the fire followed, and that the humans chose long ago.

To win you need Bulwark Ants, Thornbacks and Healing Pods.

In the ending, the inner gate falls. Varrick orders the survivors into sealed underground shelters as the command feed breaks up mid-sentence. The Bloom senses lives still beneath the stone and asks whether to follow them. The Seed Cluster says "Not now" and tells it to do what they are for: grow. The Bloom asks why they grow, and the Seed Cluster admits it does not know yet.

Survival and Base Siege

Both modes start with every growth already evolved and their own starting stock of 300 copper and 100 lead. Nothing in them touches your campaign stock or evolutions.

- Survival: a random 96×96 map. Hold your Bloom against 25 waves of HDF war machines that enter from a red zone. Waves grow from Scout Tanks to Rammer Drones, Interceptors, Flame Tanks, Bombers and Siege Tanks, with a boss every 10th wave.
- Base Siege: a large mirrored map with a full HDF colony on the far side. The colony mines, builds and pays for every unit it sends, so cutting its drills and conveyors weakens its attacks. You win by destroying its core.

The desktop app (Steam, itch.io and download)

- The desktop app is the same game in its own window, for Windows, Linux (including Steam Deck) and macOS. It opens full screen the first time. F11 or Alt+Enter switches between full screen and a window, and Settings has a Full screen switch too. The app remembers which you chose and the window's size.
- The main menu has a Quit button, and so does the pause menu (Esc) during a match.
- To install it on Windows without Steam, put LastColonySetup.exe in the folder you want the game in and run it. It downloads the newest version into that folder and adds Last Colony to the Start menu and the desktop. Running LastColonySetup.exe again, or Update Last Colony in the Start menu, gets newer versions. To remove the game, run Uninstall Last Colony (in the game's folder or the Start menu), or use Installed apps in Windows Settings. Only the game's own files are removed; anything else in the folder, and your saves, are kept. On Linux, LastColonySetup-Linux does the same, with entries in your applications menu.
- Downloaded from a zip instead: run Updater.exe (on Linux, updater) in the game's folder to get the newest version. It asks GitHub for the newest release, downloads it, checks it and replaces the game files. Your saves are not touched. Close the game first.
- Saves are kept as files in the app's own folder, so Steam Cloud can carry them between computers. The first time the app needs a save, it brings over any save an older version kept, on its own.
- Settings, then Graphics quality, has a fifth preset in the app: Ultra. It is everything in Epic, drawn at up to twice your screen's resolution for sharper edges and artwork, with denser drifting dust.
- Steady frame rate (in Settings, on at first) keeps the game smooth. If frames start to slow, it lowers the drawing resolution a step at a time, never below 75% of your screen's, and raises it again slowly once there is room. Nothing else about the picture or the game changes. Turn it off to keep the chosen resolution whatever happens.
- Press F3 to show the frame rate and the drawing resolution in use.
- On Steam, capturing each sector unlocks an achievement, and capturing every sector unlocks one more. The Steam overlay (Shift+Tab) works in the game.
